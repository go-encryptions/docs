    hmac — HMAC whose keyed state can be wiped

    github.com/go-encryptions/hmac (v0.1.0)

    import "github.com/go-encryptions/hmac"

    HMAC (RFC 2104) and PBKDF2 (RFC 8018) for Go, written from the README. crypto/hmac keeps its two padded keys inside digests a program cannot reach, so they stay in memory until the garbage collector reuses the page. hmac precomputes the same two keyed states, so Reset does not hash the pads again, but keeps them in buffers it owns and zeroes them on request. The gap is described in go-theca/theca#74.

    m := hmac.New(sha256.New, key) // implements hash.Hash; key is not retained
defer m.Wipe()                 // zeroes every key-derived byte the package holds

for _, sector := range sectors {
    m.Reset()
    m.Write(sector)
    tag = m.Sum(tag[:0])
}

dk, err := hmac.PBKDF2(sha256.New, password, salt, 500000, 64)

    The output is byte-for-byte what crypto/hmac produces, for every hash. A MAC is not safe for concurrent use. After Wipe, Write, Sum and Reset panic.

    What Wipe does, and what it cannot do

    Wipe zeroes
    the marshaled inner and outer keyed states always
    the pads when the hash cannot be marshaled
    the scratch buffer holding the last inner digest always
    the two live digests by restoring the public initial state, when the hash implements BinaryMarshaler and BinaryUnmarshaler (all of the standard library); Wipeable reports it

    Wipe cannot reach copies made by the Go runtime or the hash (Sum copies its state, the garbage collector and stack growth move values), the slice holding your key, the live digests of a hash that cannot be marshaled (they are only reset), or swap and core dumps taken before Wipe. It removes the long-lived copies the package owns; it is not a proof that no byte of the key is left in the process.

    Correctness. Known-answer vectors from RFC 4231, RFC 6070 and published PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA-256 vectors; the HMAC-SHA-256 vectors of go-theca/theca (testdata/theca_vectors.json); differential tests and fuzz targets against crypto/hmac and crypto/pbkdf2; 100% statement coverage.

    Measured, and where. Apple M4 Max only, one core, Reset + Write + Sum with one keyed MAC: 85 ns for 64 B and 1434 to 1468 ns for 4 KiB, against 85 ns and 1461 to 1469 ns for crypto/hmac. Zero allocations per operation in both. The 1 MiB and per-sector rows and other platforms are not yet measured.
